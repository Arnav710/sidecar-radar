# Conference problems: all kinds of conferences

Researched 2026-11-09. Covers tech/dev conferences (KubeCon, AWS re:Invent, Dreamforce, Web Summit, SXSW), academic (CHI, NeurIPS, science meetings), medical/CME, trade shows and association events. Sources: attendee surveys (Freeman 2025, n=2,672; CHI '25 post-conference survey, n=908; Bizzabo; MPI), Hacker News threads, Quora, Nature Careers, attendee blogs, and event-industry research.

## Scope check
The hackathon says **"Make conferences better" / "how well your project makes conferences easier"**, i.e. conferences in general. KubeCon is only the setting and the judges' home turf. So the problem should be **universal to conferences**, and the demo can *use* a KubeCon-style event as the example.

## The single most important finding
**Attendees judge a conference by personal outcomes. Organizers design for spectacle.** (Freeman 2025)
- Attendees define "experience" as: how many useful contacts they made, whether they found the right products/info, and *"Was it worth my time?"*
- Organizers define it as emotion, atmosphere, big-name keynotes and galas.
- **78% of organizers** think their event delivers memorable "peak moments". **Only 40% of attendees** agree.
- **70% of first-time attendees** don't get a peak moment. Those who *do* are **85% more likely to come back**.
- Freeman's own recommendation: **personalized guidance on which sessions to attend and who to meet**, plus personalized expo-floor guides.
- Event tech focuses overwhelmingly on organizer and exhibitor needs (registration, lead capture, analytics), **not on the attendee's own value** (Event Tech Live 2026).

→ The gap is an **attendee-side** tool that helps each person get *their* outcome.

## Ranked problems (all conference types)

### 1. Meeting the *right* people is the #1 goal, and the worst-served
- Networking is the **top reason to attend** (MPI: 52%). 51% say good networking alone is reason enough to return (Freeman/Bizzabo).
- What attendees want: **52% say "meeting professionals who face similar challenges"** is the most important factor. Speed networking (8%) and mentor sessions (13%) rank far lower.
- Reality: 95%+ of attendees want to meet new people, but unstructured formats (coffee breaks, cocktail hours) barely help. Matchmaking features run on **job titles and company size, not on what people actually need**.
- **34% cite lack of confidence** as the top reason they don't network more. A third of any room is uncomfortable, and invisible to organizers.
- It's unequal: at one IT conference, **women met 42% fewer new contacts**. Curated introductions raised women's new contacts 57% and conversation time 90%, closing the gap.
- Academic: early-career researchers find approaching senior people intimidating, and poster sessions are "crowded halls where junior researchers compete for passing attention" (FEBS, Nature).
- HN, paraphrased: networking is "like a dating site with no bios"; every chat goes job → company → awkward silence.
- **Who has it:** everyone. Universal across tech, academic, medical and trade.

### 2. You forget almost everything, and can't prove the trip was worth it
- Without follow-up, attendees forget **up to ~70% of what they heard within 24 hours**. Only **10–20% of conference learning transfers to the workplace** (Event Tech Live, citing Ebbinghaus-based research. Treat these figures as directional).
- Organizers measure satisfaction (93.5%) and attendance (79%). **Fewer than 30% measure ROI** for attendees.
- Attendees must justify the cost: "I have to prove I took something away" (HN). Toolkits tell people to write a 1–3 page trip report and present to the team. Most do it from scattered notes.
- Conversations are forgotten too: the name isn't the problem, it's *what we discussed and the next step* (Reddit).
- "Return on exhaustion": you come back tired to a full inbox, and by the time you recover, it's gone.
- **Validation signal:** at Dreamforce 2024, Salesforce built an in-app agent because event info was scattered across PDFs, websites, emails and the app. **~8,000 attendees used it to generate personalized event recaps.** People want this.
- **Who has it:** everyone sent by an employer (tech, medical CME, trade, corporate).

### 3. Too much choice, nothing personalized
- Scale everywhere: Dreamforce **2,000+ sessions**; re:Invent **2,000+**; Google Next **700+**; KubeCon **325**; CHI **5,309 attendees** with "numerous parallel sessions" as a top complaint.
- Medical/CME: first-timers are overwhelmed; advice is to pick only sessions tied to your practice and keep a backup.
- Wrong-level or misaligned content: CHI's opening keynote was "misaligned with the audience." Talks are "above or below my skill level." Sales pitches disguised as talks.
- **Who has it:** every multi-track conference.

### 4. Getting lost: navigation, expo floors, finding vendors
- Freeman 2025: **"hard-to-navigate event space" is the #1 friction point** for both attendees and organizers. For attendees, **#2 and #3 are "confusing expo floor layout" and "challenges finding vendors"**, which organizers barely noticed.
- About 30% of trade-show attendees cite a confusing expo layout. Printed floor plans go out of date.
- CHI accessibility complaints: navigation in the convention center, too little seating.
- **Who has it:** trade shows and big conferences with expos.

### 5. Full rooms, lines, and reservation chaos
- re:Invent: "session full" again and again; attendees turned away from walk-ups; an hour of queuing to be sure of a seat; reserved seats released 10 min before the start.
- SXSW 2025: a reservation system meant to cut lines made access harder, and the site crashed when reservations opened.
- NeurIPS: poster sessions closed to new entrants (2019). In 2024 the venue cap (~18k) forced a **registration lottery**.
- CHI, KubeCon: overcrowded rooms.
- **Who has it:** large conferences.

### 6. Talks are one-way; Q&A is broken
- CHI '25: frustration at the **very limited time for Q&A**. The app's question feature was underused.
- Speakers dread Q&A, and questions are often grandstanding or relevant to one person. Some cultures rarely ask questions publicly.
- Attendees value interaction: only 23% said their last event had plenty of interactive sessions (Bizzabo). Unconference/open sessions are rated more valuable than talks (HN).
- **Who has it:** every conference with talks.

### 7. Exhaustion
- Overpacked agendas are a top pet peeve (BizBash poll of 22 planners). re:Invent means **10+ miles a day** walking between hotels. KubeCon means 25–40k steps a day and "day 2 exhaustion."
- **Who has it:** large events.

### 8. Event apps and scattered information
- A new app and a new login for every event ("app fatigue"). Apps need stable internet, which conference Wi-Fi doesn't provide (CHI's app: navigation issues, login requirements, connectivity).
- Information spread across PDFs, sites, emails and Slack/Discord/Luma (Dreamforce). Virtual attendees didn't even know the conference Discord existed (CHI).
- **Who has it:** everyone.

### 9. Basics (real, but mostly not software problems)
Bad audio/AV (MPI: the most annoying thing for attendees), Wi-Fi and power outlets, signage, food lines and dietary labeling, temperature, cost/travel, accessibility (seating, quiet rooms, captions, sign language), sustainability/waste.

## Other people at a conference (also "make conferences easier")

| Role | Top pains |
|---|---|
| **Speakers** | CFP rejections with no feedback ("a letter into the void"); often unpaid, and pay travel up front; little feedback on how the talk landed; limited Q&A time |
| **Organizers** | 70% struggle to prove ROI (Bizzabo 2025); low show rates (~50% for free events); engagement; can't see the perception gap (attendees say "fine" and don't return; ~70% of first-timers don't come back) |
| **Exhibitors/sponsors** | Commonly cited: ~80% of trade-show leads never get followed up (dated CEIR-attributed figure); leads die after ~6 days; they want *relevant* visitors, not random badge scans |

Note the problems are linked. Attendees not meeting the right people = exhibitors not getting good leads = organizers not getting repeat attendance. **Fixing #1 for attendees helps all three groups.**

---

## The cross-conference pattern
1. **Before:** *What's worth my time here, and who should I meet?* (Problems 1, 3)
2. **During:** *Get me to the right room, person or booth, and help me engage, not just sit.* (4, 5, 6, 7)
3. **After:** *What did I learn, who did I meet, what do I do next, and how do I prove it?* (2)

All three are a **personal-relevance and memory** problem: matching *me* (my work, my goals) against *the event* (talks, people, booths, conversations). Underneath, that's a search/retrieval problem, which fits the Elasticsearch track, and observability and Kubernetes fit naturally as the platform.

Existing tools (Whova, Swapcard, Brella, Sched, Cvent) are organizer-first and match on titles. The Dreamforce agent proves demand, but it's locked inside one vendor's event.
